How to read the measures

Weighted degree: the sum of an actor's relationship scores. Eigenvector: connections to other actors with high connection weights.

Closeness: how short the calculated routes are to other actors. Betweenness: how often an actor lies on shortest routes between others.

Positive scores make links; zeros make no link. Empty cells are unscored. Higher scores mean greater coordination need, so path calculations use reciprocal scores. These routes describe the group's assumed network; they do not establish how information actually flows.

Eigenvector is scaled to the highest value in the current map. Closeness is calculated within each actor's reachable set. Betweenness is an undirected count of shortest-path shares. Node sizes use the separate power scores on a fixed 0–100 scale in both rounds. Node area increases with power; in 3D, an orthographic view keeps the same size at every depth. Zoom scales all nodes equally. Depth and rotation have no effect on the measures. A score of zero has a visible node. Power does not enter the centrality calculations. Line widths use a fixed relationship scale.

Low centrality does not reduce an actor's rights or legitimate concerns. The entries record your group's judgments.
